    Dual-Port Grid-Forming Control of Modular Multilevel Converters

    ← All research projects
    Research project Dual-port GFM · MMC

    Physics-based modeling and control redesign for dual-port grid-forming modular multilevel converters, validated in a back-to-back MMC Simulink system.

    Research question

    How can the internal and external power-balance mechanisms of a dual-port grid-forming MMC be represented more directly to simplify its control structure?

    Why this problem matters

    A dual-port grid-forming (GFM) modular multilevel converter can support stable operation when a voltage-forming source is available on either the AC or DC side. This flexibility is attractive for HVDC and converter-dominated systems, but conventional implementations can require relatively involved internal power-balancing loops and coordinate transformations. The project asks whether the underlying power relationships can be expressed more directly so that the controller becomes simpler without losing the essential balancing functions.

    Approach

    The work analyzes the MMC from both terminal and individual-arm perspectives. An arm-level analytical power-flow model is used to relate capacitor-energy imbalance to internal power transfer. Based on these relationships, the control structure is reorganized around direct horizontal and vertical capacitor-energy balancing mechanisms.

    The resulting design removes an unnecessary inner current loop on the AC arm-voltage control path and replaces part of the more abstract internal power-control structure with feedback relationships derived from transient arm power. The goal is not simply to reduce the number of blocks, but to make the control architecture follow the physical power-transfer mechanism more directly.

    Simulation study

    The proposed controller was implemented in a back-to-back MMC AC-HVDC-AC system in Simulink. The study examines normal balanced operation, several unbalanced operating conditions, internal circulating-power behavior, and recovery from a large transient disturbance.

    The available project materials are simulation-based.

    My role

    This project was completed during my 2022 WISPO/WEMPEC research at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. In the project draft, my contribution is described as summarizing the control structure, developing the detailed analytical model and control idea, redesigning the controller, conducting the simulations, and preparing the initial manuscript.

    Connection to later research

    This work strengthened my foundation in power-electronic dynamics, physics-based modeling, and control architecture design before my Ph.D. research shifted toward data-driven modeling and intelligent energy-system operation. The same preference for exposing useful physical structure rather than relying on unnecessarily complicated representations later became an important theme in my structure-aware modeling research.
