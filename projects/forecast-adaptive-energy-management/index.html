    Forecast-Adaptive Microgrid Energy Management

    ← All research projects
    Published MMDG-AKRR · SOCP · MPC

    Online PV forecast correction coupled with rolling energy dispatch and cone-relaxed network constraints.

    Research question

    How should a microgrid revise its operating decisions when measured PV output departs from the expected day-ahead profile?

    From a forecast to an operating decision

    A day-ahead PV forecast can become inaccurate when weather conditions differ from the expected profile. Repeating an optimization is not enough when its forecast remains systematically mismatched. This project develops a forecast-adaptive, online MPC-based energy management system for grid-connected microgrids. 1

    Multi-modal PV correction

    Multi-modal Dictionary-Guided Anisotropic Kernel Ridge Regression (MMDG-AKRR) combines models associated with representative PV operating profiles. An anisotropic kernel distinguishes heterogeneous power-history and time features, while online similarity-driven weights combine the base models using the measured input sequence.

    The resulting forecast correction is embedded into a rolling-horizon EMS. A second-order cone relaxation of the branch-flow model allows bus-voltage and branch-current limits to be represented within a tractable optimization problem.

    Evidence

    The published study compares the forecasting module with neural-network and kernel baselines. It also compares linear, cone-relaxed, and nonlinear power-flow-constrained EMS formulations on 10-, 18-, and 33-bus microgrids, assessing forecasting adaptability, operating cost, network-security performance, and computation. 1

    The contribution lies in the forecasting mechanism and its integration into constrained online operation. It does not claim that MPC or SOCP relaxation is itself a new technique. Optimization of a relaxed problem is also distinct from an unconditional guarantee for the original nonlinear AC network.

    Related work

    Frequency-aware islanded operation addresses the additional need to balance local generation, frequency support, and service impacts without main-grid support. AORF-RR load forecasting is a separate data-efficient forecasting contribution, rather than the PV model used in this paper.
