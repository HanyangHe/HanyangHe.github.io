From incomplete physics to useful dynamic models

Microgrid digital twins and predictive applications require dynamic models that remain accurate when training data are scarce and device-level equations are incomplete. Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy) offers compact, interpretable models with inexpensive coefficient estimation, but its accuracy depends strongly on the candidate-function dictionary. Expert-designed physical dictionaries can be effective, yet creating them often requires precisely the equation-level knowledge unavailable to the identifier. This research addresses how to construct informative physical candidates from less detailed engineering semantics.

LLM-assisted physical-dictionary discovery

LLM-Prior SINDy converts device type, variable meanings, coordinate conventions, and qualitative interaction cues into reusable candidate dictionaries. A pretrained LLM proposes coefficient-free physical expressions and interaction families. A deterministic compiler validates and canonicalizes the proposals, removes redundant terms, and expands physically relevant interactions. The final dictionary is evaluated by a shared sparse-regression backend, rather than having the LLM select coefficients or repeatedly search equations.

  1. 01

    Specify

    Provide device semantics, available coordinate conventions, and qualitative physical-interaction knowledge.

  2. 02

    Propose

    Use an LLM to suggest coefficient-free physical expressions and interaction families.

  3. 03

    Compile

    Validate and canonicalize symbolic candidates, then construct bounded interaction expansions.

  4. 04

    Identify

    Select sparse terms and estimate coefficients from data. Cache the dictionary for later reuse.

Modular microgrid dynamics

The framework identifies local dynamic models for synchronous generators (SGs), grid-forming (GFM) inverters, and grid-following (GFL) inverters, while retaining the known network algebraic equations of the microgrid differential-algebraic system. Devices of the same type share the discovered candidate dictionary, but their coefficients are fitted independently. The representation can therefore be reused across network sizes without making new LLM calls during identification or simulation.

Reported numerical evidence

Simulation studies use 10-, 69-, and 123-bus microgrids to evaluate short-trajectory identification, sample efficiency, measurement-noise sensitivity, and rollout reliability. Under the tested limited-data conditions, LLM-Prior SINDy achieves favorable prediction accuracy compared with generic and evolved dictionaries. Prior-level ablations indicate that the largest improvements arise from combining LLM-suggested physical expressions with deterministic interaction expansion, rather than using direct LLM proposals or generic feature expansion alone.

Online adaptation and receding-horizon prediction

The discovered dictionary also supports lightweight online model maintenance. When a device's dynamics change, a rolling mismatch detector identifies the affected module. Its active coefficients can be updated using recent observations, followed by subsequent-window validation and optional state synchronization. The dictionary and selected sparse support remain fixed during these online updates.

In a separate 69-bus sliding-start prediction study, the identified model completes 49 of 49 tested 12-s prediction windows, with mean trajectory RMSE 1.8007 × 10−8, without retraining or additional LLM calls. This is an open-loop receding-horizon prediction evaluation with prescribed future inputs, not a demonstration of closed-loop MPC.

Scope and current status

The framework assumes known network algebraic relationships and device states that can be measured or estimated. Recovering unknown internal states from partial terminal measurements, learning unknown network parameters, and establishing closed-loop control performance are outside the present study. Results are from numerical simulations rather than hardware or field deployment. These limitations motivate future work on physics-guided dynamics discovery from partial measurements and trustworthy terminal-equivalent modeling.

This work is a journal manuscript under review. Related published foundations include physics-embedded converter identification and MPC and geometric learning and simulation.
