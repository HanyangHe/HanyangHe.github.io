    Geometric Learning and Simulation of Dynamics

    ← All research projects
    Published GMKRR · GSINDy · CAW-GMLS

    Tangent-aware representations and geometry-aware time integration for learning differential equations on manifolds.

    Research question

    How can a learned vector field and its numerical integration remain consistent with the manifold geometry inferred from observed trajectories?

    Two related sources of error

    A learned derivative can contain a component pointing away from the manifold occupied by observed states. Separately, numerical integration can accumulate geometric error even when the local derivative estimate is useful. This research addresses both the representation of the vector field and its propagation through time. 1 2

    Geometrically constrained kernel learning

    The ICLR 2025 collaboration develops a geometrically constrained operator-valued kernel for learning vector fields on tangent bundles. Local geometry is estimated from data, and a compatible ODE solver approximates the exponential-map evolution. The work includes an error analysis under its stated assumptions and numerical studies of high-dimensional dynamics. 2

    Sparse identification and curvature adaptation

    Geometrically Constrained SINDy (GSINDy) incorporates estimated tangent-space constraints into sparse vector-field learning. Curvature-Adaptive Weighted Generalized Moving Least Squares (CAW-GMLS) improves local geometric estimation by adapting the weighting to curvature. A compatible normal-correction integrator addresses off-manifold deviations during prediction. 1

    The related 2024 microgrid study decomposes discrete-time state increments into tangent and normal components, combining sparse identification with local geometric reconstruction. Its numerical validation uses a ten-bus microgrid. 3

    Evidence and limitations

    The studies evaluate nonlinear trajectories, curved manifolds, and dynamics reduced from high-dimensional systems. They examine candidate-function compatibility, curvature sensitivity, noise, and off-manifold perturbations. Reported improvements are tied to these settings and to the quality of the local geometric estimate.

    Finite data and numerical approximation matter. The asymptotic results in the kernel study should not be read as exact finite-sample preservation of an unknown manifold in every application.

    Connection to adaptive models

    Geometric correction changes how a learned model is simulated. It is distinct from online digital-shadow adaptation, which updates a model after the physical system changes. The two address complementary forms of model reliability.
