    Adaptive Microgrid Digital Shadows

    ← All research projects
    In preparation PWN-SINDy

    Modular, physics-embedded identification with selective and causally validated updates when device dynamics change.

    Research question

    How can a digital shadow detect and adapt to a localized change without relearning an entire interconnected microgrid?

    A model that follows a changing system

    This project develops the dynamic-model layer of a microgrid digital shadow. Known network algebraic relationships are retained, while uncertain generator and inverter dynamics are represented as separately identifiable modules. This makes a localized change a model-update problem at the device level rather than a reason to retrain every component. [1]

    Physics-embedded random-feature identification

    Prior-weighted Neural SINDy (PWN-SINDy) combines fixed neural ridge features with explicit physics-derived candidates. Prior-weighted sparse regression encourages useful physical terms without imposing a complete governing model. The learned representation remains linear in its outer coefficients, allowing localized updates without reconstructing the feature dictionary.

    The online adaptation loop

    1. 01

      Detect

      Use a rolling mismatch score to identify an inconsistent device module.

    2. 02

      Update

      Refit its active coefficients, giving greater weight to recent observations.

    3. 03

      Validate

      Test the candidate on a subsequent observation window, not the fitting window.

    4. 04

      Synchronize

      Accept a sufficiently improved model and align it with the measured state.

    Reported simulation evidence

    The manuscript studies offline identification on 10-, 69-, and 123-bus microgrids. A reported online case introduces hidden parameter changes in one grid-forming inverter of the 69-bus system, using an initial model identified from 500 samples and 0.1% noise in derivative labels and observations.     This is one specified simulation comparison, not an across-the-board performance guarantee. Temporary error peaks remain during update-delay intervals. The manuscript also reports regimes in which physics-augmented polynomial SINDy is preferable when abundant clean data are available.

    Scope and current status

    The contribution is a data-efficient, updateable digital-shadow modeling layer. The current study focuses on identification and synchronization; integration with downstream monitoring, optimization, and control is a further research direction. The manuscript is in preparation.

    Related directions include geometry-aware simulation and frequency-aware microgrid operation.
