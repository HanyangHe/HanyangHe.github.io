    Physics-Embedded MPC for Battery Charging

    ← All research projects
    Published / early access PhD-SI · MPC

    Compact dictionary models that incorporate incomplete physics and support predictive control of a battery charging converter.

    Research question

    How can an accurate, interpretable prediction model remain inexpensive enough to train and evaluate inside a charging controller?

    Control depends on the prediction model

    A battery-charging controller must respond to changing electrical conditions while respecting operating limits. Model predictive control can explicitly represent such requirements, but its decisions depend on a useful prediction model. Incomplete physical knowledge and uncertain parameters motivate learning from measurements without discarding the physics already available. 1

    Approach

    Physics-embedded Dictionary-based System Identification (PhD-SI) incorporates physical expressions into a sparse dictionary representation. The journal formulation includes a nested linear-in-parameter structure and fractional-power monomial candidates to represent nonlinear relationships compactly.

    The model is evaluated as part of the control loop, not only as a standalone regression model. The research therefore considers training cost, prediction cost, model accuracy, and the resulting charging response together. The 2025 conference study introduces the physics-embedded dictionary-based MPC approach; the 2026 journal article develops the modeling framework further. 1 2

    Reported evidence

    Numerical charging case studies compare the proposed approach with data-driven alternatives under disturbances and parameter uncertainty. The reported results concern modeling accuracy, closed-loop control behavior, and computational efficiency.

    Validation in these studies is simulation-based. Hardware implementation and deployment are separate steps beyond the reported numerical comparisons.

    Software and publication scope

    The public EV-charging-project repository identifies the 2025 conference paper as its associated study. It is therefore linked below as the conference implementation, not as a verified reproduction package for every experiment in the 2026 journal extension.
