    Frequency-Aware Islanded Microgrid Operation

    ← All research projects
    Published + extension Adaptive reserve · Service-aware curtailment

    Coordinate frequency reserve, battery flexibility, EV charging, and load curtailment in renewable-rich islanded microgrids.

    Research question

    How can islanded energy management reduce unnecessary frequency-reserve headroom during routine operation without sacrificing frequency security, while using service-aware flexibility to manage severe generation shortages?

    Published foundation: constrained islanded dispatch

    The 2026 ITEC+EATS paper develops a convex EMS for EV-rich islanded microgrids. Its formulation incorporates voltage and branch-current limits, frequency-regulation requirements, battery state-of-charge constraints, and EV charging-session constraints with uncertain connection behavior. The numerical comparisons examine security, operating cost, and computational efficiency. 1

    Ongoing extension: adaptive reserve and service priorities

    The journal manuscript develops two complementary mechanisms within a receding-horizon EMS. They address different operating conditions rather than treating all power mismatches as the same problem. 2

    Routine intra-interval fluctuations. A data-driven frequency-response estimator and a forecast-aware safety guard adapt the scheduled regulation reserve to the operating condition. The goal is to release unnecessarily conservative reserve without degrading the observed frequency response.

    Severe generation shortages. A service-aware hierarchy coordinates BESS terminal-energy relaxation, noncritical EV charging curtailment beyond reserved commuting needs, and flexible-load shedding. The ordering makes the consequence for users part of the dispatch decision.

    Reported evaluation

    The manuscript evaluates 10-, 18-, 33-, and 69-bus islanded systems. It reports lower scheduled reserve with comparable or improved observed frequency-security performance relative to full-reserve scheduling, together with reduced direct load interruption under severe PV shortages. 2

    The learned reserve mechanism is an economical screening approach, not a formal dynamic-security certificate. The manuscript explicitly leaves more formal uncertainty and security-margin treatment to future work.

    Publication boundary

    The conference paper is published. The adaptive-reserve and service-aware extension is a manuscript under review, with a different author list. They are listed separately below so that ongoing results are not attributed to the published conference article.
