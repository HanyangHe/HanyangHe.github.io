    Data-Driven Methods for Intelligent Power and Energy Systems

    I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical Engineering at Penn State. I develop structure-aware, data-driven methods for modeling, simulating, and controlling nonlinear physical systems, with a primary focus on power and energy systems.

    My research asks how limited measurements and available physical or geometric knowledge can be combined to learn useful models, maintain their validity under changing conditions, and support reliable operating decisions.

    Research Thrusts

    Structure-Aware Learning & System Identification Learning useful predictive and dynamical models from limited data by incorporating available physical structure, prior knowledge, geometric constraints, and efficient representations.

    Slow time scales:

    • Data-scarce forecasting

    Fast dynamics:

    • Physics-embedded modeling
    • Sparse identification
    • Vector field learning with geometric constraints

    Reliable Simulation & Adaptive Models – Fast Dynamics Maintaining the validity of learned models during long-term simulation or under changing physical conditions.

    • Online system identification and adaptive digital shadows of the power system
    • Geometry-structure-aware dynamic simulation with online corrections

    Predictive Control & Energy-System Operation – Across Time Scales Using data-driven learned prediction models to support constrained decisions in intelligent energy systems.

    • Slow-time-scale decisions: MPC-based energy management and frequency regulation power reserve-aware operation
    • Fast dynamic control: MPC-based power converter control
    • Cross-scale application: Evaluating energy-management decisions through fast dynamic simulations
