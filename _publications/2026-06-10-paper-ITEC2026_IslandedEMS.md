High penetration of Electric Vehicles (EVs) introduces significant challenges to the secure and economical operation of low-inertia islanded microgrids, which has a high requirement on the Energy Management System (EMS). However, existing EMS frameworks that account for multiple dispatchable resources, EV loads with uncertain connection behaviors, and comprehensive security constraints often exhibit strong non-convexity, leading to high computational complexity. This paper develops a convex Second-Order Cone Programming (SOCP)-based EMS that explicitly incorporates critical network security constraints, including branch current limits, nodal voltage magnitude bounds, and frequency regulation constraints, along with battery State-of-Charge (SoC) constraints and EV charging session constraints with uncertain behaviors. The proposed convex formulation enhances computational efficiency while guaranteeing global optimality, thereby enabling fast and reliable scheduling for islanded microgrids with high EV penetration. Comparative studies against conventional linear-programming-based EMS, EMS with simplified security constraints, and a GA-solved nonconvex EMS demonstrate that the proposed approach provides a favorable security–economy trade-off with efficient computation.
